About

About Second Act

A blog for people 40 and over who want to build something of their own: a side income, a consulting practice, a small shop or a business they can run on their own terms.

Who writes it

Second Act is written and edited by the team at Brightfront Studio. We build and maintain websites for small, independent businesses: cafes, salons, food trucks, makers, consultants and local services. Every week we talk to owners who are just getting started, and a large share of them are starting later in life, after a first career, after raising a family, or alongside a job they intend to keep for a while.

We are not lawyers, accountants or financial advisers, and we never pretend to be. When an article touches on registering a business, taxes or insurance, we explain the general idea and tell you who to ask for advice about your own situation.

Who it is for

Readers in their 40s, 50s, 60s and beyond who have real experience but may not feel at home with online marketing, websites or new tools. We assume you are smart and busy, not that you are "bad with technology". We explain every term the first time we use it.

How we write and check articles

  • Practical first. Each article tries to answer one real question completely, with steps you can follow today.
  • No invented numbers. We avoid statistics we cannot stand behind. Where prices are mentioned, they are typical ranges and we say so.
  • Plain English. Short sentences, defined terms, no hype and no "get rich quick" promises. Most small businesses grow slowly, and we say that too.
  • Reviewed and updated. Articles carry a published date and, when we revise them, an updated date. If you spot a mistake, email info@brightfrontdesign.com and we will fix it.
  • Human-edited. We use writing tools to help with drafts, the same way we suggest you might in your own business, but every article is edited, fact-checked and approved by a person before it goes live.

How the blog is funded

The blog is free to read. It is supported in two ways: by Brightfront Studio's website service, which we mention where it is genuinely relevant, and by advertising served by Google AdSense. Ads are clearly separate from our writing, and advertisers have no say in what we write. You can read how ads and cookies work on this site in our privacy policy.
