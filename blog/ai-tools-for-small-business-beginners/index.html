How to use AI in your small business: a beginner's guide (no tech skills needed)

You don't need to understand how AI works to use it, any more than you need to understand an engine to drive a car. AI assistants can save a small business owner hours every week on writing, planning and admin. This guide shows you how to start safely, with examples you can copy.

In short

  • AI assistants are like a fast, eager helper: great at first drafts, summaries and ideas, but they make mistakes.
  • Always check facts, figures and anything legal, financial or medical before you use it.
  • Never paste in confidential customer details, passwords or sensitive business information.
  • The more context you give (who you are, who it is for, the tone you want), the better the result.

What an AI assistant actually is

An AI assistant is a program you talk to by typing (or speaking) in ordinary language. You ask a question or give it a task, and it replies in seconds, like a very fast, very well-read helper. Well-known examples include ChatGPT, Claude, Google Gemini and Microsoft Copilot. Most have a free version you can use on a website or a phone app.

The easiest way to think of it: an eager assistant on their first day. It is quick, knows a little about almost everything and never gets tired. But it does not know your business, it sometimes misunderstands, and it can state wrong things with total confidence. You are still the boss. You give clear instructions and check the work.

Getting started in 10 minutes

  1. Choose one assistant, such as one of those above, and open its website or download its official app. Be careful to use the official site or app store listing; there are many look-alike apps.
  2. Create a free account with your email address.
  3. Check the privacy settings. Many tools let you choose whether your conversations can be used to improve their systems. Choose what you are comfortable with.
  4. Type your first request in ordinary words, as if writing to a helpful colleague. Try one of the examples below.
  5. Reply to improve it. If the answer isn't right, say so: "make it shorter", "less formal", "add our opening hours", "write it for people over 60".

The secret to good results: give context

The difference between a poor answer and a useful one is almost always the instructions. A good request includes:

  • Who you are: "I run a small dog-walking business in Leeds."
  • What you want: "Write a short Facebook post."
  • Who it is for: "for busy working dog owners in my neighbourhood."
  • The goal: "to tell them I have two new weekday slots."
  • The tone and length: "friendly, not salesy, under 80 words."

Weak: "Write a post about my business."

Strong: "I run a small dog-walking business in Leeds. Write a friendly Facebook post, under 80 words, for busy dog owners in my neighbourhood, saying I have two new weekday lunchtime slots. Not salesy. End with an invitation to message me."

12 practical ways to use AI in a small business

Writing and communication

  1. Reply to emails. Paste in a customer's message (with personal details removed) and ask for a polite reply that says what you want to say.
  2. Write website text. Give it notes about your business and ask for a clear "About us" section or service descriptions. Then rewrite it in your own voice.
  3. Social media posts. Ask for a month of post ideas, or turn one idea into three short versions.
  4. Difficult messages. Chasing a late payment, declining a job, responding to a negative review. AI helps you stay calm and professional.
  5. Your Google Business Profile description. Ask for a 700-character description from your notes, then check it against our Google Business Profile guide.

Planning and thinking

  1. Brainstorm business names and then check they are not already in use.
  2. Plan a launch or promotion step by step, with a simple timeline.
  3. Prepare for a meeting: "What questions should I ask an accountant when starting a small business?"
  4. Think through pricing: describe your costs and hours and ask it to show the calculation. Check the maths yourself, and see our pricing guide.

Admin and learning

  1. Create templates and checklists: a new-client form, a cleaning checklist, a booking confirmation.
  2. Summarise long documents, like a supplier contract or a government guide, so you know what to read carefully. Always read important documents yourself before signing.
  3. Explain jargon: "Explain what 'SEO' means in plain English for a small bakery owner."

Five rules to stay safe

  1. Don't share private information. No customer names with addresses, bank details, passwords, medical details or confidential business documents. Remove or replace personal details before pasting anything in.
  2. Check every fact. AI can invent facts, statistics, quotes, laws and even references that sound real. Check anything important against a trustworthy source.
  3. Don't rely on it for professional advice. Use it to understand and prepare, then ask a qualified accountant, lawyer or adviser.
  4. Edit before you publish. Add your own experience and voice. Customers can often tell bland, unedited AI text, and it does not build trust.
  5. Watch out for scams using AI. Criminals use AI to write convincing emails and even fake voices. Be extra careful with unexpected requests for money. Our guide to scams targeting small business owners explains what to look for.

Copy-and-paste prompts to try today

"I'm starting a [type of business] in [town] for [type of customer]. Suggest 10 simple business names that are easy to say and remember. Avoid puns."

"Here are my notes about my business: [notes]. Write a clear, friendly 'About' section for my website, around 150 words, for customers in their 40s and 50s. Use short sentences and no jargon."

"A customer has not paid an invoice that was due 14 days ago. Write a polite but firm reminder email. Keep it short and professional."

"I got this review: [review]. Write a calm, professional reply that thanks them, apologises for their experience and invites them to contact me directly to sort it out."

"Create a simple one-page checklist for a new client's first appointment at my [type of business]."

Start small

You do not need to use AI for everything. Pick one task that takes too much of your time, such as emails, social posts or writing descriptions, and try an AI assistant for that alone for two weeks. If it saves you time, keep going and try the next task. If not, you have lost nothing.

Used sensibly, AI is a cheap, patient helper that gives you more time for the parts of your business that need you: your customers and your craft.

Common questions

Which AI assistant is best for a small business beginner?

The well-known general assistants, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Google Gemini and Microsoft Copilot, can all handle everyday writing and planning tasks, and most offer a free version. Try one or two with the same task and keep the one whose answers you prefer.

Is it safe to use AI for my business?

It is safe for many everyday tasks if you follow a few rules: don't share confidential or personal information, check facts before publishing, and read the privacy settings of the tool you use. Treat its answers as a draft, not the final word.

Will customers know I used AI to write something?

Text used without editing often sounds generic. If you add your own experience, examples and voice, and edit for accuracy, the result reads as yours. Being honest and accurate matters more than how the first draft was produced.

Can AI replace an accountant, lawyer or adviser?

No. AI can help you understand terms, prepare questions and organise information, but it can be confidently wrong. For tax, legal, financial or health decisions, rely on a qualified professional.
