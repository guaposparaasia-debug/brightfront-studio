How to set up a Google Business Profile: a step-by-step guide for non-tech people

When someone searches for "bakery near me" or "plumber in [your town]", the businesses that appear on the map come from Google Business Profile. It is free, it takes about an hour to set up, and for many local businesses it brings in more customers than anything else. Here is how to do it, step by step.

In short

  • A Google Business Profile is free and controls how your business appears on Google Search and Maps.
  • Use your real business name, the most accurate category and correct hours. Accuracy matters more than tricks.
  • Add good photos, a clear description and your services, then keep them up to date.
  • Reviews have a big effect on whether people choose you. Ask happy customers and reply to every review.

What is a Google Business Profile?

A Google Business Profile (it used to be called Google My Business) is the box of information that appears when people search for your business on Google, and the pin that shows on Google Maps. It shows your name, opening hours, phone number, address or service area, photos, reviews and a link to your website.

It matters because a huge share of local customers find businesses through Google Search and Maps. When someone searches for "hairdresser near me", Google usually shows a map with a few businesses at the top. Those come from Business Profiles. If you do not have one, you are not in that list.

It is free, you control it, and you can update it from your phone.

Before you start: what you need

  • A Google account. If you use Gmail, you already have one. Consider creating a separate account just for your business.
  • Your exact business name, as you use it on signs, invoices and your website.
  • Your address, if customers visit you, or a list of towns or areas you serve if you go to them.
  • Your phone number and website (if you have one).
  • Your opening hours.
  • A few good photos: outside, inside, your work, and yourself or your team.
  • About an hour of quiet time.

Step 1: Start your profile

  1. On a computer or phone, go to google.com/business and choose to get started or manage your profile.
  2. Sign in with your Google account.
  3. Type your business name. If it appears in a list, your business may already have a profile (Google sometimes creates them automatically). Select it and follow the steps to claim it. If not, choose to create a new business with that name.

Step 2: Choose your business category

Google asks you to choose a primary category, such as "Bakery", "Bookkeeping service" or "Dog walker". This is one of the most important choices, because it affects which searches you appear in.

  • Choose the most specific category that describes your main business. "Italian restaurant" is better than "Restaurant".
  • Start typing and pick from Google's suggestions. You cannot create your own categories.
  • You can add secondary categories later for other things you do, but keep them genuinely relevant.

Step 3: Location or service area

Google asks whether you have a location customers can visit.

  • If you have a shop, office or studio that customers visit, say yes and enter the address. It will appear on Google Maps.
  • If you go to customers (a gardener, cleaner, tutor who visits homes), you can choose not to show an address and instead list your service areas, such as towns, cities or postcodes. Your home address stays private.
  • If you do both, you can show your address and also list service areas.

Do not use a PO box or a virtual office you do not actually work from. Google's rules require a real location, and profiles with fake addresses can be removed.

Step 4: Add contact details

Add the phone number customers should call and your website address. If you do not have a website yet, you can leave this blank and add it later. Make sure your name, address and phone number are exactly the same as on your website and other listings. Consistency helps Google trust your information.

Step 5: Verify your business

Google needs to confirm you are the real owner before your profile goes fully live. Depending on your business, you may be offered one or more of these options:

  • Phone call or text with a code.
  • Email with a code.
  • Video recording: a short, unedited video showing your location, signage or equipment, and proof you manage the business.
  • Postcard sent to your address with a code, which can take a couple of weeks.

Google decides which options you see. Follow the instructions carefully. If verification fails, check that your details are accurate and try again, or use Google's help pages to contact support.

Verification is always free

Google does not charge for verification. If someone calls offering to "verify your Google listing" for a fee, it is very likely a scam.

Step 6: Complete your profile fully

A complete profile looks more trustworthy to customers, and Google says complete, accurate information helps you appear in relevant searches. Fill in everything that applies:

Opening hours

Add your regular hours, and use special hours for public holidays and closures. Wrong hours are one of the quickest ways to annoy a customer who turns up to a closed door.

Description

You have up to 750 characters to describe your business. Use plain language that a customer would use: what you do, who you help, where you work, and what makes you different. For example:

"Family-run bakery in the heart of Millbrook, baking sourdough, pastries and celebration cakes fresh every morning since 2024. We use local flour and seasonal fruit, and take custom cake orders with three days' notice. Wheelchair accessible, with a small seating area and good coffee."

Do not stuff it with keywords, include links, or write in capital letters.

Services or products

List your main services or products, with short descriptions and prices if you are happy to share them.

Attributes

Tick relevant attributes such as "wheelchair accessible", "free Wi-Fi", "appointment required" or "online appointments". These help people who search for specific needs.

Booking or ordering links

If you use an online booking or ordering system, add the link so customers can book straight from Google.

Step 7: Add good photos

Photos make a big difference to whether people click on and choose a business. Aim for:

  • A clear logo and a cover photo.
  • The outside of your premises, so people recognise it when they arrive.
  • The inside, showing what it feels like to visit.
  • Your work or products: dishes, haircuts, finished gardens, before-and-after shots.
  • You and your team. People like to see who they will be dealing with.

Phone photos are fine. Take them in daylight, hold the phone steady, keep the background tidy and avoid heavy filters. Add a few new photos every month or two.

Step 8: Get and respond to reviews

Reviews are one of the main things people look at when choosing between local businesses. Your profile gives you a link you can share to ask for reviews. Send it to happy customers by text or email shortly after you finish a job.

  • Reply to every review, briefly and politely. Thank people for positive reviews.
  • For negative reviews, stay calm, apologise for their experience, and offer to sort it out privately. Future customers read your reply as much as the review.
  • Never post fake reviews or pay for reviews. It is against Google's policies and can lead to reviews being removed or your profile being restricted.

Step 9: Keep it up to date

Your profile is not a one-time job. A few minutes a month keeps it working for you:

  • Update hours for holidays and any changes.
  • Add new photos.
  • Reply to new reviews and answer customer questions.
  • Post updates for news, offers or events.
  • Check that Google has not accepted incorrect edits suggested by the public. Anyone can suggest changes to a business listing, so look at your profile regularly.

You can manage your profile from your phone by searching for your business name on Google while signed in, or by using Google Maps.

Common questions

Is Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating, verifying and updating a Google Business Profile is free. Be wary of anyone who calls claiming you must pay Google to stay listed or to verify your business; see our guide to common scams targeting small business owners.

Can I have a Google Business Profile without a shop or office?

Yes. If you serve customers at their location, such as a plumber, cleaner or mobile hairdresser, you can set up a service-area business. You list the areas you cover and keep your home address hidden from the public.

How long does Google verification take?

It varies. Some businesses are verified within minutes by phone, text, email or a short video. Others take several days, especially if a postcard or a manual review is needed. Google decides which methods are offered to you.

Do I still need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?

A profile is a great start, but a website adds a lot: full details of your services and prices, your story, answers to common questions and a place that you control. The profile can link to your website, and the two together build more trust than either alone.

Can I put keywords in my business name to rank higher?

No. Google's guidelines say your profile name should be your real-world business name. Adding extra keywords or locations can lead to your profile being suspended.
