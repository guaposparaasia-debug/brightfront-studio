Simple tech

How to build a simple website for your small business (without hiring a developer)

A small business website does not need to be big, clever or expensive. It needs to answer a stranger's questions quickly: what you do, whether you can help them, roughly what it costs and how to get in touch. You can build a site like that yourself, in plain steps, without learning to code.

In short

  • Most small businesses need just four things on their website: a home page, services, about and contact.
  • A website builder is usually the simplest choice for a beginner. WordPress is more flexible but asks more of you.
  • Write in plain words, show real prices or starting prices, and make contacting you effortless.
  • Check every page on your phone, link the site to your Google Business Profile, and keep it up to date.

What a simple website needs to do

Think about the moment someone finds your website. They have heard your name from a neighbour, or seen you on Google Maps, and they want to know three things before they pick up the phone: Can you help me? Can I afford you? How do I get in touch?

A good small business website answers those questions within a minute, on a phone, without making anyone hunt. That is the whole job. It does not need animations, a blog, a shop or dozens of pages. Many excellent local businesses run on a site of one to four pages.

Keeping that job in mind will save you hours. Whenever you are unsure whether to add something, ask: does this help a customer decide to contact me?

The four pages most small businesses need

You can build these as four separate pages, or as four sections of one long page. For a small local business, either works. One page is quicker to build and easy to read on a phone; separate pages give you more room as you grow.

Home

Who you are, what you do, who you help and where, plus one clear button to get in touch.

Services

What you offer, what is included, and prices or starting prices.

About

Your experience, why you started, and a friendly photo of you.

Contact

Phone, email, a simple form, your area or address, and your hours.

If you sell products, you might add a page of photos and prices. If customers always ask the same questions, a short "Common questions" section helps. But start with the four above and get them right first.

Website builder or WordPress? A plain-English comparison

These are the two most common ways to build a site yourself. Neither is "right" for everyone; it depends on how much you want to manage.

Website builders and WordPress compared for a small business beginner
Website builderWordPress (self-hosted)
What it isAn all-in-one service: design, hosting and editing in one placeFree software you install on hosting you rent separately
Ease for a beginnerUsually easier: drag, drop and typeMore to learn, more choices to make
Hosting and security updatesHandled by the builderYour responsibility, or your host's or helper's
FlexibilityLimited to what the builder offersVery flexible, with many themes and plugins
How you payA monthly or yearly planHosting, plus any paid themes or plugins
Moving away laterCan be harder to move your site elsewhereEasier to move between hosts

For most people starting a small service business or shop, a website builder is the calmer choice: you can focus on your words and photos, and someone else keeps the technical side running. WordPress makes more sense if you want lots of control, plan to grow the site significantly, or already know someone who can look after it.

Try before you commit

Most website builders let you start building for free and only ask you to pay when you publish or connect your own domain. Spend an evening trying two of them before you decide. Choose the one that feels easiest to use, not the one with the most features.

Domain name basics

Your domain is your address on the web, such as yourbusiness.com. You rent it by the year from a domain registrar, or through your website builder. A few simple rules:

  • Short and easy to say out loud. You will say it on the phone and print it on cards.
  • Close to your business name. Avoid hyphens and unusual spellings.
  • Choose a familiar ending, such as .com or your country's own ending.
  • Register it in your own name and keep the login safe, even if someone else builds the site.
  • Turn on auto-renew with your registrar, so it never lapses by accident.

Expect letters and emails offering to "renew" your domain from companies you have never dealt with. Many are not genuine. Our guide to scams that target new small business owners explains how to spot them.

Once you have a domain, you can usually set up a matching email address, such as hello@yourbusiness.com, which looks more professional than a free webmail address.

What to write on each page

The words matter more than the design. Write the way you speak to customers: short sentences, no jargon, and specific details.

Home page

  • One clear headline saying what you do and for whom: "Garden care for busy homeowners in Millbrook and nearby villages."
  • Two or three short lines on why people choose you: experience, reliability, insurance, friendly service.
  • One main button, such as "Call for a free quote" or "Book a visit".

Services page

  • Each service with a short description of what is included.
  • Prices or "from" prices. Visitors who cannot see a price often assume it is too high. Our guide on how to price your services helps you set them.
  • The area you cover, and anything you don't do, to save wasted enquiries.

About page

  • Your experience in plain terms: "Twenty years as a nurse before training as a care companion."
  • Why you started the business and what you care about.
  • A real, friendly photo of you. People like to see who they will be dealing with.

Contact page

  • Phone number and email, written out in full and tappable on a phone.
  • A short contact form asking only for what you need.
  • Your hours, and how quickly you usually reply.
  • Your address and a map if customers visit, or the towns you cover if you go to them.

If writing is not your strength, an AI assistant can help with first drafts. Our beginner's guide to AI tools includes a prompt for writing an "About" section. Always edit the result so it sounds like you and every fact is right.

Photos: real beats perfect

Good photos make a small website look trustworthy. Phone photos are fine if you take them in daylight, hold the phone steady and keep the background tidy. Show your real work, your premises if you have them, and yourself. Avoid generic stock photos where you can; customers can usually tell, and they want to see the real business.

Make sure it works on a phone

Many of your visitors will find you on a phone, often while standing in a street or sitting in a car. Most modern builders and themes adjust to small screens automatically, but always check yourself:

  • Open every page on your own phone, not just the computer preview.
  • Is the text big enough to read without zooming?
  • Does tapping the phone number start a call?
  • Are buttons large enough to tap easily?
  • Do pages load quickly? Very large photos are the usual cause of slow pages, so resize them before uploading if your builder doesn't.
  • Ask a friend with a different phone to try it too.

Connect your site to your Google Business Profile

A free Google Business Profile is how you appear on Google Maps and in local search results. Your website and your profile work best as a pair:

  1. Add your website address to your Google Business Profile.
  2. Use exactly the same business name, phone number and address on both, so customers and search engines aren't confused.
  3. Keep your hours matching, including holidays.
  4. Link to your Google reviews from your website, or mention them, once you have a few.

If you haven't set up your profile yet, follow our step-by-step Google Business Profile guide.

What it costs, in general terms

A do-it-yourself website is one of the cheaper parts of starting a business, but it isn't free. Typical costs include:

  • A domain name, paid yearly.
  • A website builder plan, paid monthly or yearly, or hosting if you use WordPress, plus any paid themes or plugins.
  • Optional extras such as a professional email address, online booking or a paid photo shoot.
  • Your time: building the site takes evenings, and keeping it up to date takes a little time every month.

Prices vary by provider, plan and country, so compare a few before you choose. For how a website fits alongside your other start-up costs, see how much it really costs to start a small business.

Before you publish: a final checklist

  • Every page says clearly what you do and where.
  • Prices or starting prices are visible.
  • Phone, email and contact form all work. Send yourself a test message.
  • Hours, address or service area are correct and match your Google Business Profile.
  • Spelling checked, ideally by someone else.
  • Every page checked on a phone.
  • Your domain is set to auto-renew, and you know your login details.

Then publish it. A simple, accurate site that is live this month is far more useful than a perfect one that is still unfinished next year. Tell people about it: our guide to getting your first customers shows how.

Keeping it up to date

An out-of-date website can do more harm than none: wrong hours, old prices or a closed phone line send customers elsewhere. Put a reminder in your calendar once a month to check your hours, prices and photos, and update anything that has changed.

When it makes sense to get help

Doing it yourself is a perfectly good way to start. It can make sense to hand it to someone else later if:

  • You keep putting off updates because the website feels like a chore.
  • Your time is now worth more spent on customers than on the website.
  • You need something beyond a simple site, such as online ordering, bookings or many pages.
  • The site looks dated or doesn't work well on phones, and you aren't sure how to fix it.

If you do get help, ask what is included, who owns the domain and the content, and what updates cost after launch. Whichever route you choose, the goal is the same: a clear, honest site that helps a stranger decide to get in touch.

Common questions

Can I build a business website myself with no technical skills?

Yes. Modern website builders let you choose a design, type your words, add photos and publish, without writing code. The hardest part is usually deciding what to say, not the technology.

Should I use a website builder or WordPress?

For most beginners, a website builder is simpler: hosting, security and updates are handled for you. WordPress gives you more control and choice, but you are responsible for more of the setup and upkeep, or you pay someone to look after it.

How much does a simple small business website cost?

Costs vary by provider and plan. Typically you pay for a domain name each year and either a monthly or yearly plan for a website builder, or hosting plus any paid themes or plugins for WordPress. Your own time is the other real cost. See how much it really costs to start a small business for the wider picture.

Do I need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?

A Google Business Profile helps people find you on Google Search and Maps. A website gives them the full picture: your services, prices, story and answers to common questions, on a page you control. The two work best together.
