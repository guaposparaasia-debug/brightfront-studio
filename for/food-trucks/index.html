The "where are you today?" problem

A restaurant has one address. A food truck or market stall has a schedule, and it changes with the weather, the season, events and permits. That makes the most important question your customers have, "where can I find you?", also the hardest one to answer on a website that nobody updates.

The honest solution depends on how you work. If you have regular spots, like the same corner every Tuesday or a weekly farmers market, the website can show that pattern as a simple weekly schedule. If your location changes every day, it is better for the website to say so clearly and point people to the social account where you post today's spot, rather than showing a schedule that is wrong half the time. Many trucks do both: a regular pattern plus "check Instagram for today".

The sections a food truck site usually needs

Where to find us

Regular spots and days, markets you attend, or a clear link to where you post daily locations.

Menu

A short, readable menu. Trucks usually do a few things very well, so the list can be short.

Photos

The truck itself so people spot it from across the street, the food, and the window on a busy day.

Catering and events

What you offer for private events, festivals and office lunches, and what to send in an inquiry.

About

Who you are and why you started. Truck owners usually have a good story.

Follow us

Links to the accounts where you post daily updates, so the website and your feed work together.

Catering and events: where the bigger orders come from

For many trucks, private events and catering are a large part of the business, and they usually start with someone searching for food trucks for an event and landing on a website. Make it easy for them to send a useful inquiry. A good catering section explains what kinds of events you do, whether there is a minimum, roughly how far you travel, and what information you need to quote: the date, the location, the number of guests, the time window and any dietary needs. Your exact terms and minimums are yours to set; the website simply presents them clearly and gives people a way to contact you.

Menus for a moving kitchen

Keep the online menu short and honest. If items rotate, list the regulars and add a line such as "plus weekly specials on Instagram". Mark vegetarian or other dietary options if you have them, since people often look for those before choosing where to eat. Prices are optional; many trucks show them because it speeds up the line.

Seasons, weather and changing hours

Trucks and stalls often run different hours in winter, close for a few weeks in the off-season or pause for big events. Those are exactly the changes the $39/month maintenance covers. Email us the new schedule or a line like "closed until March" and we update the site, so it never tells people you are somewhere you are not.

Market stalls and pop-ups

If you sell at markets or run pop-ups, the same ideas apply: list the markets and days you attend, link to the market's own page when it has one, and say how people can order ahead or reach you between markets. A small stall with a clear one-page website looks established, which helps when you apply for new markets or approach shops about wholesale.

Common questions from food truck owners

My location changes every day. Is a website still useful?

Yes. The website holds everything that does not change daily, like your menu, photos, catering information and contact details, and it points people to the account where you post today's location.

Can people book us for events through the website?

The website explains what you offer and gives people a clear way to contact you, by phone, email or message. We can also link to a booking or inquiry tool you already use.

We close for winter. What happens to the website?

It stays online and says when you are back, so people do not think you have closed for good. Updating it is covered by maintenance.

Do we need a long menu online?

No. A short list of your regular items, with a note about specials, is usually best on a phone.

Can the site show our weekly schedule?

Yes, if you have a regular pattern. If it changes often, we recommend a simpler note plus a link to where you post updates, so the site never shows outdated information.

Is this different from a market stall website?

Not much. Stalls list the markets and days they attend instead of street spots. Everything else, menu, photos, contact, works the same way.
