What new clients check before they book

Choosing a stylist or barber is personal. Before someone sits in your chair for the first time, they want to be reassured on a few points: that you do the kind of work they want, that the price is within their budget, that they can book easily, and that the place feels right. Your website is where they look for that reassurance, often late in the evening, often on their phone, and usually after seeing you on Instagram or in a map search.

A salon website does not need to be big. It needs to be clear, current and easy to act on. The best ones make booking the obvious next step on every screen.

The sections a salon site usually needs

Welcome and booking button

Your name, a photo of your best work or your space, and a "Book now" button above the fold.

Services and prices

A tidy list of services with prices or starting prices and how long each one takes.

Gallery of your work

Real cuts, colors, fades and styles, shot in good light. This is what sells.

The team

Short introductions if clients book a specific stylist or barber, with what each person specializes in.

Policies

Deposits, cancellations, late arrivals, walk-ins: written once, kindly, so nobody is surprised.

Location and hours

Address, parking or transit tips, accessibility, and opening hours per day.

Writing a price list people trust

Price is one of the first things a new client wants to know, and one of the hardest for salons to publish because so much depends on hair length, thickness and the work involved. A few approaches work well:

  • "From" prices. Show a starting price for each service and explain briefly what can raise it, such as length, thickness or a correction.
  • Time as well as price. "Cut and blow-dry, about 60 minutes" helps people plan and shows you do not rush.
  • Group by service type. Cuts, color, treatments, styling, barbering, extras. Short groups are easier to scan on a phone.
  • Consultations. If some services always need a consultation first, say so and make it easy to request one.

Booking: link the system you already use

Most salons and barbershops already use a booking app. We do not replace it. We put a clear "Book now" button on the site that opens your existing booking page, and we repeat it where it is useful, for example next to services and at the end of the page. If you take appointments by phone or message instead, we use a tap-to-call or message button. Barbershops that welcome walk-ins can say so clearly, along with the usual quiet times if you want to share them.

Showing your work, the right way

Your gallery does more selling than any sentence on the page. Photograph finished work in soft, even light, against a plain background, from more than one angle when it helps. Before and after pairs can be powerful for color and correction work. Always ask clients before posting their photos, especially when their face is visible. If you already post your work on Instagram, you can send us your favorites from there, and we link to your Instagram so people can see the latest.

Policies, written kindly

Clear policies prevent awkward conversations. Many salons publish a short note on deposits, cancellation notice, lateness and children's appointments. Keep the tone friendly and the rules short. We help you word them clearly, but the policies themselves are yours to decide.

Common questions from salon and barbershop owners

Will the website replace my booking app?

No. We link to the booking app you already use so clients can book in one tap. Your app keeps handling appointments, reminders and payments, with its own fees.

Should I list exact prices?

Showing at least starting prices helps new clients decide. You can explain what changes the final price, and update the list by email whenever it changes.

Can each stylist have a profile?

Yes, if it helps your clients. A short introduction, a photo if they want one, and what they specialize in is usually enough.

Can I use photos from my Instagram?

Yes. Send us your favorites, and make sure the clients in them are happy to be shown. We also link to your Instagram so people can see your newest work.

Is Instagram not enough for a salon?

Instagram is excellent for showing your work. A website adds a place that shows up in Google searches, works for people without the app, and keeps services, prices and booking together.

We are a barbershop that takes walk-ins. What should the site say?

Say it clearly near the top, with your hours. If you also take appointments, show both options so nobody has to guess.
