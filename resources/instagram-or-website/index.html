Do I need a website if I already have Instagram?

Short answer: keep your Instagram, and add a simple website if people regularly ask you for your hours, prices or menu. The two do different jobs.

Updated November 2026 · Brightfront Studio

What Instagram does well

  • Shows your newest work, dishes or products, fast.
  • Lets regulars follow you and see what's new today.
  • Messages, comments and stories make it feel personal.

What a website adds

  • It opens for everyone. No app, no account and no login wall. Someone who doesn't use Instagram can still see your menu.
  • It answers the practical questions. Hours, address, prices, services and how to book, in one place that doesn't scroll away.
  • It shows up in searches. People searching for "bakery near me" or "barber in [your town]" often land on websites and map listings. A site with your real text gives search engines more to work with.
  • You control it. A platform can change its rules, its feed or your account. Your website stays where you put it.
  • It looks established. To a lot of first-time customers, a clean website is a sign you're a real, open business.

When Instagram alone is probably fine

If you're brand new, testing an idea, or nearly all your customers already find you through people they know, Instagram may be enough for now. When you notice the same questions in your messages every week, that's usually the sign a website would save you time.

How the two work together

Put your website link in your Instagram bio, and link back to your Instagram from the site. Instagram keeps things fresh day to day. The website holds the facts that don't change every day. Most small shops only need one page to do that, see one page or several pages.
