Getting found

Do I need a website if I already have Instagram?

Short answer: keep your Instagram, and add a simple website if people regularly ask you for your hours, prices or menu, or if you want to be found by people searching on Google and maps. The two do different jobs.

In short

  • Instagram is for showing what is new. A website is for answering the same questions reliably.
  • A website opens for everyone, with or without the app, and gives search engines your details as text.
  • Use both: link them to each other and keep your hours consistent everywhere.

What Instagram does well

  • Shows your newest work, dishes or products, fast, with photos and video.
  • Lets regulars follow you and see what is new today, which is perfect for specials and sold-out notices.
  • Messages, comments and stories make the business feel personal.
  • It is free to use and quick to update from your phone.

For many small shops, Instagram is where the personality lives. Nothing here suggests replacing it.

Where Instagram falls short

Instagram is built for scrolling, not for looking things up. The details a new customer needs, like opening hours, prices, address, parking or how to book, end up buried in a bio, a pinned post, a highlight or a caption from months ago. People who do not use Instagram, or who are not logged in, often hit a login wall after a few seconds. And the feed is controlled by the platform: what your followers see depends on an algorithm, and an account problem can make your page disappear overnight.

What a website adds

  • It opens for everyone. No app, no account and no login wall. Someone who does not use Instagram can still see your menu.
  • It answers the practical questions. Hours, address, prices, services and how to book, in one place that does not scroll away.
  • It shows up in searches. People searching for "bakery near me" or "barber in [your town]" often land on websites and map listings. A site with your real details written as text gives search engines more to work with.
  • You control it. A platform can change its rules, its feed or your account. Your website stays where you put it.
  • It looks established. To many first-time customers, a clean website is a sign of a real, open business.

When Instagram alone is probably fine

If you are brand new, testing an idea, or nearly all your customers already find you through people they know, Instagram may be enough for now. When you notice the same questions in your messages every week, like "what time do you close?" or "how much is a haircut?", that is usually the sign a website would save you time.

How to make them work together

  1. Put your website link in your Instagram bio.
  2. Link back to your Instagram from the website, so people can see what is new.
  3. Keep the facts that rarely change, like your hours, menu, prices and address, on the website.
  4. Use Instagram for what changes daily: specials, new work, sold-out notices, today's location for food trucks.
  5. Keep your hours identical everywhere. When they disagree, customers do not know which to trust.

What to put in your Instagram bio

Your bio is the bridge between the two. Keep it to the essentials: what you are, where you are, and the link to your website. For example: "Family bakery in the old town. Open Tue to Sun. Menu, hours and cake orders at the link below." Once the website holds the details, your bio no longer has to squeeze in hours, prices and policies, and your posts can focus on what is new.

The minimum website worth having

You do not need a big site. One page with your name, a few good photos, what you offer, hours, location and a call or book button covers almost everything a new customer needs. Common questions

Can my website show my latest Instagram posts?

We link your website to your Instagram so people can see your newest posts there. Keeping the website focused on the facts that rarely change, and Instagram on what is new, works best for most small shops.

Will a website take followers away from Instagram?

No. Linking the two usually helps both: people who find you on Google discover your Instagram, and followers have one place to check your hours and prices.

Is a Google Business Profile not enough?

It is very useful and worth keeping complete. A website adds space for your full menu or price list, your story and your booking links, in a layout you control.

Do I need to post differently once I have a website?

Not really. Keep posting as you do now, and mention the website when people ask about hours, prices or booking.
