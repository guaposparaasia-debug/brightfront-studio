Cost

What a small shop website costs, line by line

Website prices are confusing because people quote different bundles. Break any offer into the same handful of lines and they become easy to compare. Here is every line, what it is, whether you need it, and how our price maps onto it.

In short

  • Every website has the same basic cost lines: a domain, hosting, the build, content, and ongoing updates.
  • The biggest hidden cost is usually not money but the owner's time spent building and updating.
  • Compare offers by their total over two or three years, not by the first invoice.

Why website prices are so hard to compare

One offer says "websites from a few hundred dollars". Another says "no monthly fees". A website builder advertises a low monthly plan. A local agency quotes several thousand. They are rarely selling the same thing. Some include hosting and some do not. Some include writing, some expect you to write everything. Some include changes after launch, and some charge for each one. The only fair way to compare is to break every offer into the same lines and add them up over the same period.

Line 1: the domain name

The domain is your address on the web, like yourshop.com. It is rented by the year from a domain registrar, and almost every option, whether a builder, a freelancer or us, treats it as a separate cost that you pay to the registrar. If you already own a domain, keep it; it can usually be pointed at a new website. If you do not have one, choose a name that is short, easy to say out loud and close to your shop's name.

Do you need it? For a business, yes. A free subdomain such as yourshop.somebuilder.com looks temporary and is hard to remember.

Line 2: hosting and security

Hosting is the computer that serves your site to visitors. Security today means at minimum HTTPS, the padlock in the browser, which modern browsers expect. With a website builder, hosting is part of the subscription. With many freelancers, hosting is a separate monthly or yearly bill that you set up and pay yourself, and you are responsible for renewing it. With us, hosting and HTTPS are included in the $39/month.

Do you need it? Yes, every live website is hosted somewhere. The question is only who pays and who manages it.

Line 3: design and build

This is the work of arranging your content into a page that looks good, loads quickly and works on phones. It is the line with the widest price range, because it depends on how custom the work is and how much the builder handles for you. A template you fill in yourself costs the least in money and the most in time. Custom design by an agency costs the most. Our build is $500 in total, paid in two halves: $250 to start and $250 after you have seen the finished site and approved it.

Do you need it? You need someone to do it, even if that someone is you.

Line 4: writing

Words are the part most owners underestimate. Someone has to write the welcome, describe your services or menu, and explain hours, policies and how to book. Builders leave this entirely to you. Some freelancers charge for copywriting separately. We write the text from the notes you send, so you do not need to be a writer; you just need to know your business, which you already do.

Line 5: photos

Good photos make a small website look professional. You have three options: your own phone photos, a professional photo shoot, or stock images. Phone photos taken in daylight are perfectly good for most small shops and have one big advantage: they show your real place. A professional shoot is a separate cost if you want one. Stock photos are cheap but generic, and customers can tell. We work with the photos you send, cropping and sizing them so the page stays fast.

Line 6: updates after launch

This is where offers differ the most, and where costs quietly add up. Hours change, menus change, prices go up, staff come and go. On a builder, you make every change yourself. With a freelancer who charges per update, each change is a small invoice and a wait. With us, updates and changes you ask for are included in the $39/month, so there is no reason to leave the site out of date.

Line 7: tools you might add

Many shops use outside services for bookings, online ordering, delivery or email newsletters. These come with their own fees, paid to those companies, whichever website option you choose. A website typically links to them rather than replacing them. Taking payments directly on your own website, like a full online shop, is a different and bigger kind of project.

Line 8: your time

The line that never appears on an invoice is the time you spend. Building a site yourself takes evenings. Keeping it accurate takes a little time every month, plus the mental load of remembering to do it. If you would happily spend that time, a builder is a fine choice. If every hour on the website is an hour not spent running the shop, paying someone to handle it often costs less overall.

Questions to ask before you pay anyone

  • What is the total cost for the first year, and for each year after?
  • Is hosting included? Who renews it, and what happens if it lapses?
  • Who writes the text? Who prepares the photos?
  • Are changes after launch included, or billed one by one?
  • Is there a contract, and how do I cancel?
  • What happens to my site and my domain if I stop paying?
