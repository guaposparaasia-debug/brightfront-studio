Checklist

What to prepare before you ask for a website

You do not need everything on this list to get started, but the more of it you have ready, the faster your website comes together and the fewer back-and-forth emails you will need. Work through it in one sitting with a coffee.

In short

  • Gather the facts customers ask for most: hours, location, contact, and what you sell.
  • Phone photos are enough. Aim for your space, your best products and your front door.
  • Write a few honest sentences in your own words; someone else can polish them.

1. The basics

These are the facts every visitor needs. Write them down exactly as you want them to appear, and check them twice. A wrong phone number on a website is worse than no website.

  • Your business name, spelled exactly as you want it shown
  • Full street address, plus a note on how to find the entrance if it is not obvious
  • Phone number customers should call, and whether you answer it during opening hours
  • The email address you want customers to use, if any
  • Your area or neighborhood, as locals would describe it

2. Opening hours

List your regular hours for each day of the week, including the days you are closed. Then think about exceptions: public holidays, seasonal hours, a lunch break, early closing on certain days. If your hours vary a lot, decide where you will announce changes, for example on Instagram, so the website can point there.

  • Regular hours, day by day
  • Closed days and public holiday policy
  • Seasonal or temporary changes you already know about

3. What you sell or offer

For a restaurant or cafe this is your menu. For a salon it is your service list. For a shop it might be your main product categories. You do not need to type it nicely; a clear photo of your current menu or price list is enough for someone to work from.

  • Your current menu, service list or product categories
  • Prices, or a decision to leave them off
  • Your best-sellers or signature items, the three things you want people to try
  • Dietary, accessibility or other details customers often ask about

4. Photos

Photos do more than words on a small business website. Take them in daylight, keep the lens clean, and skip heavy filters. Here is a simple shot list:

  • The outside or front door, so people recognize you
  • The inside when it looks its best: tidy, well lit, maybe with a customer or two (with their permission)
  • Three to five of your best products, dishes or pieces of work
  • You or your team at work, if you are comfortable with it
  • Your logo, if you have one (no logo is fine)

Only send photos you took yourself or have permission to use. Photos copied from other websites can create copyright problems later.

5. Your story, in a few sentences

Customers like to know who is behind a local business. Write three to five sentences answering: who runs it, how long you have been open, what you care about and who your regular customers are. Do not worry about style. A few honest lines in your own words make a better starting point than a polished paragraph that could describe any shop.

"We're a family-run bakery that opened in 2015. Everything is baked here every morning, and our sourdough sells out by noon on Saturdays. Most of our customers are neighbors who stop in on the way to work."

6. Links and accounts

  • Instagram, Facebook, TikTok or other social accounts you actively use
  • Your Google Business Profile link, if you have one
  • Booking, ordering or delivery pages you want customers to use
  • Your existing website or domain name, if you already have one

7. Decisions to make first

A few choices shape the whole website. Thinking about them before the build saves rework later.

  • What is the one action you want visitors to take? Call, book, order, visit or follow. The page will be organized around it.
  • Show prices or not? Prices build trust and reduce questions. If they change often, you can show starting prices or leave them off.
  • One page or several? Most small shops are well served by one page. See one page or several pages.
  • Domain name. Keep the one you have, or choose a short name that is easy to say out loud.
  • Policies. Deposits, cancellations, returns, walk-ins: decide what you want to say before it goes on the page.

8. Things you can skip for now

Do not let an incomplete list stop you. You can start without a logo, without professional photos, without a perfect menu and without a long story. With maintenance included, adding things later is a quick email rather than a new project. The essentials are your name, contact details, hours, what you offer and a few photos.
