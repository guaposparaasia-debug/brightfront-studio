Why websites need maintenance at all

A website is not a printed sign. It lives on a server that has to stay online, it needs a security certificate that has to keep working, and browsers and phones change every year. More importantly for a small shop, the information on it goes out of date: hours shift, menus change, prices rise, staff come and go. A website that is not maintained slowly becomes a liability, telling customers you are open when you are not, or showing a menu from two years ago.

What's included

  • Hosting and HTTPS. Your website is hosted for you and served over HTTPS, the padlock in the browser. No servers or hosting accounts for you to manage.
  • Keeping it current. We keep the site working and up to date, so it keeps loading quickly and displaying correctly.
  • Updates to your information. Hours, holiday closures, menus, prices, photos and text.
  • Changes on request. Email us what you would like different and we make the change.
  • Mobile-friendly, always. Changes are made to look right on phones, tablets and desktops.
  • A person to talk to. Email info@brightfrontdesign.com and someone from our team answers.

Examples of changes you might send

  • "We're closed Monday for the holiday."
  • "New fall menu attached, please swap it in."
  • "Prices for cuts are going up next month."
  • "Here are three new photos of the shop."
  • "We started taking bookings online, here's the link."
  • "Jamie has joined the team. Here's a short bio."
  • "We're doing a pop-up at the market this Saturday, can you add it?"

How to ask for a change

  1. Email info@brightfrontdesign.com with what you want changed. Be as specific as you can, for example "Change Saturday hours to 9 to 3".
  2. Attach anything new: photos, a new menu, a price list or a link.
  3. We make the change, check it on a phone and reply to let you know it is live.

A tip that saves time: if several things are changing at once, send them in one email. And if a change has a start date, like new prices from the first of the month, say so.

It also helps to tell us how you would like the change to appear. "Add a line under the menu saying the kitchen closes at 2" is quicker to get right than "mention the kitchen hours somewhere". If you are not sure, describe the problem, for example "people keep arriving after the kitchen closes", and we will suggest where it belongs.

What counts as bigger work

Maintenance is designed for keeping your site accurate and making the changes a shop needs over time. Occasionally owners want something much larger, like a complete redesign or a brand-new kind of feature. If you are planning something much bigger than an update, email us first and we will tell you up front what is involved before anything starts.

When it starts and how to stop

You pay $250 to start the build and $250 after you approve the finished website. The $39/month starts once your site is approved and live. It is month to month, so you can cancel anytime with no contract.

Keep your other listings in sync

Your website is one of several places customers check. When you send us a change, it is worth updating the same detail on your Google Business Profile and your social accounts at the same time, especially hours. When all three agree, customers trust the information. When they disagree, people call to ask, or they simply go somewhere else. A simple habit helps: whenever you email us a change, take two minutes to update your other listings too.

Maintenance compared with doing it yourself

On a website builder, the equivalent of maintenance is you: logging in, finding the right block, editing, checking it on your phone, and remembering to do it at all. That is fine if you enjoy it. For many owners, the real value of maintenance is that updating the website becomes a one-line email sent from the counter, not a job for the weekend.
