The three common ways to get a website

1. Build it yourself on a website builder

You pay the builder a monthly or yearly subscription, pick a template and do the writing, photos and layout yourself. The subscription keeps the site online. Every later change, like new hours, a new menu or a new price, is also yours to make.

2. Pay a freelancer once, then per change

Many low-cost freelancers advertise a flat build price with "no monthly fee". That's real, but hosting usually still has to be paid by someone, and updates are often billed one at a time. A shop that changes its menu or hours a few times a year pays for each of those.

3. A build fee plus a small monthly that includes changes

That's how we work: $250 to start, $250 after you approve the finished site, then $39/month that covers hosting, updates and changes you ask for. No per-change fees, and it's month to month.

The cost people forget: the owner's time

A builder can look cheapest because your time isn't on the bill. Setting up a decent site usually takes an evening or several, and then someone has to keep it right. If you value an hour of your time at even a modest rate, a few hours a month of fiddling with menus and photos adds up quickly. It also tends not to happen in a busy week, which is how websites end up showing last year's hours.

If you enjoy building things and have the time, a builder is a perfectly good choice. If you'd rather spend that time running the shop, paying someone who includes changes is often the better deal.

Questions to ask any provider

  • What is the total for the first year, and for each year after?
  • Are changes included, or billed one by one? What counts as a change?
  • Who pays for hosting, and is it included?
  • Is there a contract, or can I cancel month to month?
  • What happens to the site and my domain name if I stop paying?
