Why starting from home makes sense

Many small businesses start at a kitchen table. There is a good reason for that. Working from home lets you:

  • Keep costs low. No shop or office rent, and you can often use furniture and equipment you already own.
  • Start small and test your idea before you spend serious money.
  • Fit work around your life, whether that means caring for family, keeping a part-time job or simply choosing your own hours.
  • Skip the commute, which saves time, money and energy.

Step 1: Check the rules before you start

This is the step most people skip. A short check now can save trouble later. The rules depend on where you live, so treat this as a list of questions to ask, not legal advice.

  • Local council or city rules. Some places need a home business permit or a simple form, especially if customers visit, you put up a sign, or you use a lot of parking. Look on your council or city website for "home business".
  • Your lease or mortgage. If you rent, check your rental agreement or ask your landlord. Some mortgages also have conditions about business use.
  • Housing rules. If you live in a flat or a planned community, there may be a homeowners' group or residents' rules that limit business use.
  • Food rules. If you make food to sell, you may need to register with your council or health department and follow food safety rules. Some places have special "cottage food" rules for home kitchens.
  • Home insurance. Tell your insurer. Many home policies do not cover business equipment, stock or visiting customers.
  • Tax registration. Like any business, you will need to report what you earn. Our main guide explains the basics of paperwork for a new business.
When in doubt, ask

A quick call or email to your council, landlord or insurer is usually all it takes. Most home businesses that keep things quiet and small run into very few problems.

Step 2: Set up a proper work space

You do not need a separate office, but you do need a place that is yours for work. It helps you focus, keeps your things organised, and makes it easier to stop at the end of the day.

  • Pick one spot. A spare room is ideal, but a corner of a room or a set part of the kitchen table can work.
  • Keep work things together in a box, a shelf or a cupboard you can close.
  • Get a good chair and good light. If you will sit for hours, your back will thank you.
  • Check your internet. If you will be on video calls or sending files, a reliable connection matters.
  • Think about noise. Plan calls for quiet times, and let the household know when you need peace.

If you make things, keep work materials and stock away from family food and living areas. This is often a rule for food businesses, and it is sensible for everyone.

Step 3: Keep business money separate

When the business is at home, it is easy for business and household money to get mixed up. Keeping them apart from day one makes life much easier.

  1. Open a separate bank account for the business, even a simple second account to start with.
  2. Pay all business costs from that account, and pay all business income into it.
  3. Keep receipts for anything you buy for the business. A folder or a photo on your phone is fine.
  4. Ask an accountant about home costs. In many places you may be able to claim part of your heating, power or internet if you use part of your home for work. The rules vary, so get advice.
  5. Set aside money for tax from each payment you receive.

Step 4: Protect your home address and privacy

When you run a business from home, your business address is also where you live. You do not have to share it with the world.

  • Use a mailbox service for post if you do not want your home address on letters, invoices or your website. This is a company that gives you a postal address and passes your mail on.
  • List your town or service area online instead of your street address, if customers do not need to visit. Your Google Business Profile lets you hide your address and show the areas you serve. Our Google Business Profile guide explains how.
  • Use a separate phone number for the business, so you can switch it off at night.
  • Share your address only when needed, for example when a customer books a visit.

Step 5: If customers visit your home

Some home businesses, like lessons or beauty treatments, mean people come to you. A little planning keeps everyone safe and comfortable.

  • Book visits in advance, so you know who is coming and when.
  • Keep a tidy, welcoming space near the door, separate from the rest of the home if you can.
  • Think about parking and neighbours. Lots of cars or noise can upset the people next door.
  • Keep pets away during appointments, unless your customers expect and welcome them.
  • Check your insurance covers visitors. This is often called public liability insurance. It covers you if someone is hurt, or their things are damaged, at your home.
  • Trust your instincts. If a booking feels wrong, it is fine to say no or to meet in a public place instead.

Step 6: Keep work and home life apart

The hardest part of working from home is often not the work. It is stopping. When your work is always in the next room, it is easy to keep going all evening. It is just as easy to let household jobs take over your working day.

  • Set working hours and tell family and customers what they are.
  • Have a "start" and "finish" habit, such as a short walk or putting your laptop away.
  • Dress for work, even simply. It helps your mind switch on and off.
  • Take proper breaks and a full lunch away from your desk.
  • Leave the house each day, even briefly. Working from home can feel lonely.
  • Talk to other small business owners, online or at a local group, so you are not on your own.

Step 7: Look professional from home

Customers do not need to know, or mind, that you work from home. They want you to be clear, reliable and easy to reach.

  • A simple website with what you do, prices and how to get in touch. See how to build a simple website.
  • A professional email address on your own web address, rather than a free one.
  • Quick, polite replies to messages, within your stated hours.
  • A quiet, tidy background for video calls.
  • A clear business name. Our guide to choosing a business name helps.

A simple checklist to get started

  • Choose a business that suits a home
  • Check council rules, your lease or mortgage, and any housing rules
  • Tell your insurer and add business cover if needed
  • Set up a work space you can close at the end of the day
  • Open a separate business bank account
  • Decide how you will protect your address and phone number
  • Set your working hours
  • Set up a simple website and a Google Business Profile
  • Tell people you know what you now do
