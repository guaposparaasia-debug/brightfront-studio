How to choose from this list

Read through the ideas and mark any that make you think "I could do that" or "people already ask me about that". Then check three things for each one:

  • Demand: do people near you (or online) already pay for this?
  • Fit: does it suit the hours, energy and income you want? If you would like to work from home, see how to start a small business from home.
  • Start-up cost: can you test it for a small amount of money?

Cost guides below are rough: Low means you can usually start for a few hundred dollars or less, Medium means some equipment, training or stock, and Higher means significant investment. Your local costs and rules will vary. When you have a favourite idea, our plain-English breakdown of start-up costs shows how to add up your own real total.

If you have professional or office experience

1. Consulting in your field

Sell the knowledge you built over a career to smaller companies that cannot afford a full-time expert. Operations, HR, marketing, procurement, health and safety, IT, finance: almost every field has consultants. Cost: Low. Your first clients are often former employers or contacts. Our consulting guide covers this in depth.

2. Bookkeeping

Small businesses hate their books. If you are organised and good with numbers, you can keep records for local shops and tradespeople. Some areas require certification or registration for certain tax work, so check what applies. Cost: Low to Medium (software, training).

3. Virtual assistant or office manager for hire

Handle email, scheduling, invoices, travel and admin for busy owners, remotely and on a monthly retainer. Cost: Low. Reliability and discretion are what clients value most, and they are things experienced people tend to have.

4. Career and interview coaching

If you have hired people, you know what makes a strong CV and interview. Help people changing careers, returning to work or applying for promotions. Cost: Low.

5. Grant and proposal writing

Charities, schools and small companies need help writing funding applications and tenders. Strong writing and patience with detail are the main requirements. Cost: Low.

6. Training and workshops

Turn your expertise into half-day workshops for companies or the public: leadership for new managers, Excel for beginners, customer service, first aid (with the proper qualification). Cost: Low to Medium.

If you are good with people

7. Tutoring

Academic subjects, test preparation, adult literacy, or a second language. Parents pay well for patient, experienced tutors. Background checks are often expected for working with children. Cost: Low.

8. Music, art or craft lessons

Teach the instrument you have played for 30 years, or watercolour, pottery, knitting or woodwork, at home, in a community hall or online. Cost: Low to Medium.

9. Senior companionship and errands

Many families need someone reliable to visit an elderly parent, take them to appointments or help with shopping. Check licensing rules: personal care often needs certification, while companionship and errands may not. Cost: Low.

10. Tour guide or local experiences

If you know your town's history, food or walking trails, offer small-group tours for visitors. Cost: Low. Insurance is important here.

11. Fitness for older adults

With a recognised qualification, run gentle fitness, walking groups, yoga or balance classes for people your own age, who often prefer an instructor who understands them. Cost: Medium (qualification, insurance).

If you are good with your hands

12. Handyman and small repairs

Hanging shelves, fixing doors, assembling furniture, minor plumbing. Demand is strong because many tradespeople do not want small jobs. Check which jobs need a licensed trade in your area. Cost: Medium (tools, insurance, transport).

13. Furniture restoration and upcycling

Buy pieces cheaply, restore them and sell at markets or online, or restore customers' own furniture. Cost: Low to Medium.

14. Garden design and maintenance

From weekly upkeep to designing small gardens. Older homeowners are often keen customers. Cost: Medium.

15. Home organising and decluttering

Help people sort, clear and organise their homes, especially before a move, after a bereavement or when downsizing. It takes tact and patience more than equipment. Cost: Low.

16. Moving help for downsizers

A growing niche: helping older people plan and manage a move to a smaller home, from sorting belongings to coordinating removal firms. Cost: Low.

17. Sewing, alterations and repairs

Good alteration services are hard to find. Hemming, repairs and alterations from home can build a loyal local following. Cost: Low to Medium.

If you love food

18. Home baking or preserves

Celebration cakes, bread, jams and chutneys sold at markets, to cafes or by order. Many places have "cottage food" rules that allow some home production, but check registration and labelling requirements carefully. Cost: Low to Medium.

19. Small-scale catering

Party food, office lunches or meal preparation for busy families. Usually requires a food business registration and sometimes a licensed kitchen. Cost: Medium.

20. Cooking classes

Teach the cuisine you know best, in your kitchen, a rented space or online. Cost: Low to Medium.

21. Market stall or food truck

A step up in cost and effort, but a great way to test a food idea before opening a cafe. Cost: Medium to Higher.

If you want low-contact or flexible work

22. Pet sitting and dog walking

Steady demand, flexible hours, and good for staying active. Insurance and good references are key. Cost: Low.

23. House sitting and property checks

Look after homes while owners travel, or check on holiday homes and rental properties. Trust and a local reputation are everything. Cost: Low.

24. Proofreading and editing

If you have a sharp eye for detail, edit reports, websites, books and academic work. Mostly remote. Cost: Low.

25. Selling collectibles or vintage items

Turn knowledge of antiques, books, records, stamps or vintage clothing into a business buying at sales and reselling online or at fairs. Cost: Low to Medium.

How to test your favourite idea this month

  1. Write a one-sentence offer. "I help [who] with [what] so they can [result]." For example: "I help small cafes keep their books tidy so tax time is painless."
  2. Set an introductory price. Fair, not free. Free work tells you nothing about demand.
  3. Tell 20 people. A personal message, not a mass post. Ask them to pass it on.
  4. Do the work brilliantly for the first two or three customers.
  5. Ask for feedback and a review. Then decide: keep going, adjust, or try the next idea.
