The nine parts of a one-page plan

The form follows the same structure as our guide to writing a simple business plan. Each part answers one question a careful friend, a bank or your future self would ask:

  • What, who and where describe the business in a way anyone can understand.
  • Problem and solution explain why customers will pay, and why they'll pay you.
  • First customers turns "marketing" into names and actions.
  • Startup budget shows how much money you need before you open.
  • First 90 days gives you a short list of steps you can start on Monday.

After you print it

Pin it somewhere you'll see it. Every three months, read it again, cross out what turned out to be wrong and write what you learned. A plan you update is worth far more than a long one you never open.

Common questions

Is one page really enough?

For most small businesses starting out, yes. A one-page plan makes you answer the questions that matter: what you sell, to whom, why they will buy and how you will reach them. If a bank or investor asks for more, it becomes the outline for the longer version.

How do I save my plan as a PDF?

Click Print my plan and choose Save as PDF (or Microsoft Print to PDF) as the printer. Only the plan is printed, on a single page.

Is what I type stored anywhere?

Your answers stay in your browser. They are saved on this device only so you don't lose them if you reload the page, and they are never sent to us. Use Clear form to remove them.

What if I don't know an answer yet?

Write your best guess and mark it as one. A plan with honest guesses is more useful than a blank space, and the three-month review is where you replace guesses with facts.
