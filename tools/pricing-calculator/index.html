How the calculator works

First it works out the full cost of one job: what you spend on it, plus your time at your target hourly rate, plus a fair share of your monthly overhead (overhead ÷ jobs per month). Charging exactly that amount means you break even and pay yourself, with nothing left over for the business.

Then it adds profit as a margin on the price: price = full cost ÷ (1 − margin). It shows your chosen margin as the suggested price, with a lower price 10 points below and a higher price 10 points above, so you can see what each option means for your month.

A worked example

Say a job uses $20 of materials and takes 2 hours, you want $40 an hour, your overhead is $400 a month and you do 20 jobs a month. Your time costs $80, your overhead share is $20, so the full cost is $120. At a 30% margin the price is $120 ÷ 0.7 = $171.43, which leaves $51.43 profit per job, or $1,028.57 a month on top of your $1,600 pay.

Before you publish a price

  • Check the market. Look up what three to five similar businesses near you charge. Your price does not have to be the cheapest.
  • Round it. $170 or $175 reads better than $171.43.
  • Review it every few months. Costs go up; your prices should too.

Common questions

Why is my time counted as a cost?

Because it is one. If you leave your own pay out, a price can look profitable while you are really working for free. The calculator pays you your target hourly rate first; the profit it shows is extra money for the business on top of your pay.

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin is profit as a share of the price. Markup is profit as a share of the cost. A 30% margin on a $120 cost gives a price of about $171.43, while a 30% markup would give only $156. This calculator uses margin.

What counts as monthly overhead?

Costs you pay whether or not you do a job: rent, insurance, software, phone, your website plan, accounting and similar. The calculator spreads them across your jobs per month.

Which of the three prices should I choose?

Start from the suggested price, then compare it with what similar businesses near you charge. If you are new and building reviews, the lower price can help; if you have strong experience or a waiting list, the higher price is often fair.

Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is sent to us or stored on a server.
